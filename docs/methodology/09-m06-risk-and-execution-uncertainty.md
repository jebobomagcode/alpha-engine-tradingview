## 2. Risk-input and evidence contract

**INDEPENDENT_EXTENSION.** Evaluate a versioned proposal at decision cutoff $d$. Carry input units, source, effective time, first availability, and revision. Values may be observed, modeling assumptions, explicitly unknown, or inapplicable. Separate mathematical completeness, operational feasibility and observed execution.

| Input group | Required information and meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Instrument | Venue, product namespace, exact symbol, quote, base, margin and settlement currencies, order quantity and payout units, product rule version. Identify each price basis such as mark, index, last, bid/ask or assumed fill. |
| Setup provenance | Setup and contact episode IDs, branch/profile, context, selected zone version, reaction onset/close/availability, R2 policy, evidence version and evidence class. Distinguish geometric overlap from observed price inside the zone. |
| Clock and lifecycle | Current state and reasons, decision cutoff, included observation revisions, expiry and applicable prior cutoffs. Later revisions can cause reassessment without rewriting historical decisions. |
| Entry proposal | Direction, anchor or range, trigger predicate and price basis, earliest admissible activation, expiry/time-in-force hypothesis and order type where needed. Separate trigger observation from order availability and filled price/size. |
| Protection and exits | Structural invalidation condition and price basis, protective trigger, hypothetical stop behavior, target branch/levels, partial allocations, residual and trailing policy or unknowns. |
| Account-risk inputs | Quantity and unit, budget or explicit equity and fraction, account currency, fee assets and conversions, scenario costs, and margin mode and tier when assessing leveraged feasibility. Public product data supplies no private balances. |

**M05 handoff.** Waiting, provisional, insufficient, invalidated, expired, superseded and unresolved reassessment states cannot initiate a current qualified risk plan. Earlier qualifications may be shown as historical. A currently active qualified reaction may enter risk assessment. RISK_INCOMPLETE marks inputs required at the requested layer. Contradictions and known filter failures retain explicit reasons. CANDIDATE_FOR_REVIEW can describe a plan under declared prerequisites at cutoff $d$. It conveys no executable instruction or fill. [M05 states](07-mtf-context-conflicts-and-causal-states.md) · [Stable lifecycle](08-cross-module-causal-reconciliation.md)

An after-close policy starts eligibility after the requisite final close is available. A market fill may occur later at another price. Passive-limit and stop-entry hypotheses need an activation clock, trigger basis where relevant, and an execution model. A high/low from a bar that began before the entry could be active cannot establish a subsequent trigger or fill without further sequence evidence.

## 3. Geometry, sizing and dimensional checks

Let side $s=+1$ for long or $s=-1$ for short. Let $E$ be a proposed entry, $S$ a stop reference and $T$ a target. In the positive-price scope of this study, nominal stop distance $d_p=s(E-S)$ and favorable distance $g_p=s(T-E)$ must both be strictly positive. Gross reward-to-risk is $g_p/d_p$, corresponding to Coach’s 1:2 notation for one risk unit per two reward units. Zero distance makes the ratio undefined. Taking an absolute value can hide wrong-side stops or targets.

With a support interval $[a,b]$ and long p10 interpretation, require $S<a$ under a strict outside-zone policy. Short off resistance requires $S>b$. An extra tick or volatility buffer is independent. A close beyond structural support and a stop trigger on mark or trade price are distinct predicates. Preserve the zone and cutoff version that were known at setup. A redraw cannot change earlier risk inputs.

A structural target is the next qualified level or a specified boundary of an interval on the favorable side. Fixed gross multiple $\rho>0$ gives $T=E+s\rho d_p$ before rounding or costs. A nearer structural obstacle can conflict with the fixed-R target. Show both branches and the conflict. Clipping, prioritizing, or skipping requires a declared policy. Pair entry-range endpoints with the same modeled stop/target scenario so extrema do not combine incompatible assumptions.

For a linear product with order quantity $q$ and multiplier $m$ underlying units per order unit, gross loss to the stop reference is $q m d_p$ in quote currency. For quantity directly in base units, $m=1$. Unit check is order units × underlying units per order unit × quote per underlying unit. With conversion $x$ account currency per quote unit, the same-clock modeled account loss is $x q m d_p$. Convert cash flows at separately declared rates if fees, funding or exits use different assets or dates. The fraction $d_p/E$ measures price movement, with account equity loss requiring quantity, conversion and equity.

As an independent inverse illustration, with face amount $M$ quote currency per contract, entry $E$ and exit $F$ in quote per underlying, gross P&L is $s q M(1/E-1/F)$ in underlying currency. This is the signed difference of reciprocal conversions. The formula is a hypothetical contrast and carries no attribution to the three observed USDⓈ-M records.

For hypothetical budget $B$ and fixed linear distances/units, $q\le B/(x m d_p)$ is a nominal bound. If a fraction $r$ of equity $A$ is explicitly supplied, $B=rA$ at the declared cutoff. For a positive zero-origin quantity step $\Delta q$, rounding down gives $\Delta q\lfloor q/\Delta q\rfloor$. Check applicable min/max quantity, notional and product limits, including position and exit legs. A different grid origin requires its own rule. Distinguish base-quantity from quote-sized order inputs. A quote budget alone cannot establish a filled base quantity. If no feasible positive size fits the budget, abstain. Rounding up to satisfy a minimum can violate the risk bound.

Price quantization depends on the role and direction. Rounding a stop farther beyond a zone can preserve geometry while increasing nominal risk. Rounding to the same tick can erase a valid pre-rounding distance. Recompute after rounding, including side, zone, reward, account risk, fees and filters. Recheck each partial leg and residual quantity. [Product filter receipts](../research/phase2-m06-binance-public-metadata.md)

For a specified finite stress family $H$, a hypothetical account-loss constraint is $\max_{h\in H}[-\Pi_h(q)]_+\le B$ plus balance, filters and margin feasibility. Costs and slippage may be nonlinear in size and time. A per-unit sizing shortcut requires proportionality. The finite family supplies a conditional bound for those paths. Gap or liquidity loss outside the family remains unresolved.

## 4. Costs, paths and liquidation uncertainty

### Fill and exit-path accounting

For a fully opened linear position at assumed entry fill $F_e$ with exit-leg fills $q_j$ at $F_j$, gross quote P&L on the closed quantity is $s m\sum_j q_j(F_j-F_e)$. For multiple entry fills, retain fill inventory and a declared cost basis. State when protection attaches and to which filled quantity. A partially filled entry leaves an unfilled order remainder and a smaller open position. A reduce-only, linked or cancellation assumption requires product-specific evidence. Allocations must reconcile to quantity opened, with any asset-deducted fee identified. An open residual remains exposure and can have separately labeled mark-based valuation. Full target fractions plus a named residual allocation cannot exceed one.

For hypothetical full target fills, the weighted gross outcome uses the stated fraction at each target. A remainder stopped at $S$ contributes an adverse amount on that path. Listing multiple targets does not imply equal allocation or that all fill. A trailing or break-even move requires an activation event, basis, update policy and later availability. The original Coach examples leave those details open.

- With execution-price or adverse-fill assumptions, the fill differences already contain the price effect of spread and slippage relative to a reference. Avoid subtracting it again. With mid/reference prices, model crossing the spread and incremental impact or use a named adverse price envelope. A top-of-book quote alone gives no later fill or queue guarantee.
- With proportional quote-valued fees, modeled fill fee is $f_i q_i m F_i$. Record liquidity role, fee schedule, fee asset, minimums/rebates and conversion. Fees deducted in base can reduce exit inventory. An order label does not establish the actual liquidity role.
- Under the documented positive-rate-long-pays convention, signed perpetual funding cost is $s\sum_k r_k N_k$, with absolute mark-based notional $N_k$ actually held at each applicable settlement and signed realized rate $r_k$. A negative result is a credit. An interval, floor or cap does not specify realized funding. Spot margin borrowing interest and collateral rules require a separate product model. [Binance funding methodology](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/detail/360033525031)

### Order and path ambiguity

A stop-market trigger can face a gap, spread, insufficient depth, partial execution or protection logic. A stop-limit trigger can activate and leave an unfilled remainder. When a bar crosses both stop and target for a position already active at open, distinct price paths can share the same OHLC and reverse which level is reached first. Even known first-touch order does not establish quantity execution. Lower-timeframe evidence can narrow ambiguity without establishing queue or native receipt. TradingView’s broker emulator and Bar Magnifier define simulation behavior. Their output does not provide exchange fill evidence. [TradingView strategy documentation](https://www.tradingview.com/pine-script-docs/concepts/strategies/)

USDⓈ-M conditional order documentation distinguishes mark and contract price triggers and product-dependent trigger protection. Preserve chart basis, trigger basis, execution-price basis and margin-mark basis independently. Commodity TradFi underlying-session and index regimes require their own cost and basis assumptions. [Binance conditional documentation](https://developers.binance.com/en/docs/catalog/core-trading-derivatives-trading-usd-s-m-futures/api/rest-api/trade) · [Product and session receipts](../research/phase2-m06-binance-public-metadata.md)

### Margin and liquidation

For an illustrative isolated linear notional $N$ and leverage $L$, initial margin is approximately $N/L$ before adjustments and reserves. At fixed quantity, changing $L$ changes that collateral requirement while leaving nominal price loss unchanged. At fixed collateral, higher leverage can enable higher quantity and larger nominal price loss. Maintenance depends on mark-based exposure, bracket, mode and exchange rules. Cross margin adds other positions and collateral dependencies. A liquidation assessment needs current tier/mode, collateral, position and basis evidence. An entry, stop and leverage alone cannot establish liquidation price or whether liquidation precedes stop execution. [Binance USDⓈ-M liquidation methodology](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/detail/b3c689c1f50a44cabb3a84e663b81d93) · [Public metadata limits](../research/phase2-m06-binance-public-metadata.md)

## 5. Expectancy and break-even

With complete mutually exclusive win/loss outcomes, gross positive win $G$, positive loss $D$, and per-outcome costs $C_W,C_L$ in the same currency, define net win $W=G-C_W$ and net loss magnitude $L=D+C_L$. For $W,L>0$, net reward-to-risk for those two fully specified outcomes is $W/L$, conditional arithmetic expectancy is $pW-(1-p)L$, and break-even is $L/(W+L)$. With zero costs and $G=2D$, this is 1/3. A modeled ₱10 cost in each branch on ₱200/₱100 gross changes the threshold to $110/300$, or 36.67%. If $W\le0$ and $L>0$, the two-branch model offers no positive expectancy for any $p\in[0,1]$. A nominal RR cannot repair it.

For variable exits, partition by explicitly defined outcomes, use their conditional mean P&L values, and include no-fill, partial, flat or unresolved outcomes when relevant. A probability conditioned on filled candidates cannot be applied to all proposed setups without a selection model. Fee/funding direction may differ by path. The identity for expected P&L needs a defined distribution or scenario weights to produce a numerical value. Coach’s examples, frozen timing counts, and paper stress paths supply none.

Compounding follows the chosen sizing convention. In L03, the next twenty-trade block uses 2% of the capital at the block start and keeps that cash risk within the block. A per-trade fixed-fraction thought experiment instead multiplies factors such as $1+2r$ and $1-r$ under exact full outcomes, no costs and unconstrained sizing. Neither arithmetic break-even nor a short illustrative sequence establishes long-term growth, drawdown or a future win rate.
